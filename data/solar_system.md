# The Solar System

The Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago from a giant cloud of gas and dust. At its centre is the Sun, a star that contains more than 99.8 percent of all the mass in the Solar System. Everything else, including the planets, moons, asteroids and comets, orbits around the Sun because of its gravity.

## The Planets

There are eight planets. In order from the Sun they are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. A simple way to remember the order is the sentence "My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Noodles".

The four inner planets, Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars, are called terrestrial or rocky planets. They are small and have solid surfaces. The four outer planets are giants. Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Uranus and Neptune are ice giants that contain water, ammonia and methane ice.

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. Venus is the hottest planet, with a surface temperature of about 465 degrees Celsius, because its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect. Mars is called the Red Planet because iron oxide, or rust, on its surface gives it a red colour. Jupiter is the largest planet; more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it. Saturn is famous for its bright rings made of ice and rock.

## Pluto and Dwarf Planets

Pluto was called the ninth planet until 2006. In that year the International Astronomical Union defined a planet as a body that orbits the Sun, is round, and has cleared its orbit of other objects. Pluto has not cleared its orbit, so it was reclassified as a dwarf planet. Other dwarf planets include Eris, Ceres, Haumea and Makemake.

## Moons and Small Bodies

Earth has one moon. Jupiter and Saturn each have dozens of known moons. Titan, a moon of Saturn, has a thick atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane. The asteroid belt lies between Mars and Jupiter. Comets are icy bodies that grow glowing tails when they come close to the Sun.

## Light and Distance

Sunlight takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth. The average distance between Earth and the Sun is about 150 million kilometres, which astronomers call one astronomical unit (AU).
