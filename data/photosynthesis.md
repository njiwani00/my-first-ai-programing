# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae and some bacteria use to turn light energy into chemical energy stored in sugar. It takes place mainly in the leaves, inside tiny structures called chloroplasts. Chloroplasts contain a green pigment called chlorophyll, which absorbs red and blue light and reflects green light. That is why most leaves look green.

## The Equation

The overall chemical equation for photosynthesis is:
6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2.
In words, carbon dioxide and water, using energy from light, produce glucose and oxygen. The oxygen is released into the air through small pores in the leaf called stomata.

## Two Stages

Photosynthesis happens in two stages. The light-dependent reactions take place in the thylakoid membranes of the chloroplast. They use light to split water molecules, release oxygen, and make the energy carriers ATP and NADPH.

The light-independent reactions, also called the Calvin cycle, take place in the stroma of the chloroplast. They use ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide from the air into glucose. The enzyme RuBisCO performs the carbon fixation step, and it is thought to be the most abundant protein on Earth.

## Factors Affecting the Rate

Three main factors limit how fast photosynthesis happens: light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature. Increasing any of them speeds up the process until another factor becomes the limiting factor. Very high temperatures slow photosynthesis down because the enzymes start to lose their shape.

## Why It Matters

Photosynthesis produces almost all of the oxygen in our atmosphere and is the starting point of nearly every food chain. The glucose made by plants is used for energy in respiration, stored as starch, or turned into cellulose to build cell walls.
